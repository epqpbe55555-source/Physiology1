Core rules, not a lecture

Everything worth remembering, compressed. Read this before diagrams, cards, or the quiz.

Nervous System — basics

  • Includes all neural tissue in the body.
  • Functional unit = the neuron.
  • Neurons are supported by neuroglia (glial cells), which separate & protect them.
  • Organs of the NS: brain, spinal cord, receptors, nerves.

CNS vs PNS

  • CNS = brain + spinal cord → integrates, processes & coordinates sensory data and motor commands; seat of intelligence, memory, learning, emotion.
  • PNS = all neural tissue outside the CNS → delivers sensory info to the CNS, carries motor commands to the periphery.

PNS divisions

  • Afferent → sensory → receptors → CNS.
  • Efferent → motor → CNS → effectors.
    • Somatic (SNS) → skeletal muscle.
    • Autonomic (ANS) → smooth/cardiac muscle & glands.
      • Sympathetic → fight or flight.
      • Parasympathetic → rest & housekeeping.

Resting membrane potential

  • ECF is high in Na⁺, Cl⁻.
  • ICF is high in K⁺ and negatively charged proteins.
  • Ions cross the membrane only through channels — never freely through the lipid bilayer.
  • Resting potential ≈ -70 mV.

Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase pump

  • Ejects 3 Na⁺ out for every 2 K⁺ in.
  • Active transport — requires ATP.
  • Compensates for slow Na⁺ leak in / K⁺ leak out.
  • Net effect: contributes to the negativity of the transmembrane potential.

Ion channel types

  • Leak (passive) — always open; permeability shifts with local conditions.
  • Chemically gated — open on ligand binding (e.g., ACh at the NMJ).
  • Voltage gated — open/close with membrane potential change; key to the action potential.
  • Mechanically gated — open with membrane distortion (touch, pressure, vibration).

Graded (local) potentials

  • Localized change in Vm that cannot spread far from the stimulus site.
  • Depolarization → shift toward 0 mV or above (Na⁺ enters).
  • Hyperpolarization → shift more negative, e.g. -80 mV (K⁺ leaves).
  • Spreads via local current; effect decays with distance.
  • Stronger stimulus → more channels open → larger, wider effect.
